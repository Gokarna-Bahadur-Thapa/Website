Engineering Nepal’s Energy Surplus

Mechanical engineer in Kathmandu, working on the models, feasibility studies and policy analysis that decide what Nepal does with the power it cannot yet use.

I build the demand models and feasibility studies behind Nepal’s energy efficiency, hydrogen and carbon pricing plans, for the Alternative Energy Promotion Centre, UNDP, the Water and Energy Commission Secretariat and World Bank–supported programmes. Alongside that I publish peer-reviewed research on energy storage, industrial backup power and energy access under climate shocks. I work in LEAP, MAED and HOMER Pro.

Selected work

Turning six municipalities’ waste into power

Six Nepali municipalities want to burn or digest their rubbish to make electricity instead of dumping it. I led the winning technical proposal and now run the studies that establish how much waste there is and whether the plants would work.

Modelling Nepal’s energy demand to 2035

I built the LEAP model that projects how much energy each part of Nepal’s economy will use over the next decade, and how much of it could be saved, for the government’s Sector-Wise Energy Efficiency Action Plan.

Costing three ways to price carbon in Nepal

For UNDP I compared a carbon tax, an emissions trading scheme and a hybrid of the two across four sectors, and estimated what each would raise and who would pay.
